# Harvest or keep growing: criteria used for whiteleg shrimp

Researched 2026-11-02. Question: what criteria do farmers, extension services and aquaculture economics sources use to decide when to harvest L. vannamei or keep growing, and what are the limits of average size alone?

## Answer
- No source gives a hard "harvest when ADG falls below X". Sources describe harvest as an economic decision on target size, price by size, growth, survival, risk and buyer needs [2][13].
- Vietnam (Quang Ninh, 2026): 90-110 days, economic harvest at 16-30 g; reference weights about 4, 14 and 25 g at day 30, 60 and 90 [1].
- Slow-growth numbers found: a vendor guide says ADG under 0.18 g/day after day 45 is a stall warning [13]; a Mexican farm study set a limit reference point of 0.79-1.03 g/week against a target of 1.52 g/week [9].
- Economics: more days pay while the value of weight gained beats feed cost; later days gain less and need more feed per gram because FCR rises with size [8]. The break-even formula below is derived by me, not quoted.
- Price steps by size matter more than the daily gain: Vietnam prices jump most between 40, 30 and 20 per kg [12]. Honduras data show an extra 4 weeks paid in one case and lost money in another [5].
- Risk triggers: harvest early when many shrimp are diseased or dying; "better a low price for undersize shrimp than wait too long" [7]. Cold or hot water slows growth [8].
- Sampling: weekly after about day 30-60, 50-100 shrimp or several cast-net stations, use a 3-4 week moving average [1][6][7].
- Average size alone misses molt stage, size spread, sex differences, survival and cast-net bias (see limits).

## Economics of one more day
No source states a formula; the closest is: farmers should grow larger shrimp because "the additional value of weight gain for large shrimp is more than the additional cost incurred" [11], and in Honduras extending a cycle from 17 to 21 weeks added 848 USD/ha gross (price rose with count class) for 202 feed and 56 handling, net +571; in another scenario the same extension cut net from 573 to 46 because yield stayed flat and price fell [5].

Derived by me (not from a source), feed cost only:
- Let B = biomass, w = average weight (g), g = ADG (g/day), f = daily ration as a fraction of biomass, F = feed price per kg, P = shrimp price per kg.
- Daily feed cost = f x B x F. Daily value gained = B x (g / w) x P (same size class).
- Break-even ADG: g = w x f x F / P. Equivalent: marginal FCR (f x w / g) must stay below P / F.
- Vietnam example: F = 35,000 VND/kg [12]. At w = 25 g, f = 1.5 percent [1], P = 130,000 VND/kg (40 per kg) [12]: g = 25 x 0.015 x 35 / 130 = about 0.10 g/day (0.7 g/week). At w = 20 g, f = 2.1 percent, P = 125,000: about 0.12 g/day.
- Not in the formula: power, labour, interest, mortality risk (each 1 percent of animals lost per day removes 1 percent of value), price steps at count boundaries (50/kg to 40/kg: +4 percent price; 40/kg to 30/kg: +23 percent [12]), and a price that moves daily.
- Per-shrimp value (derived from [12]): 20 g = 2,500 VND; 25 g = 3,250; 33 g = 5,300.

## Limits of average size alone
- Cast-net samples favour smaller, slower-escaping shrimp; catch varies with moon, water and crew; use one crew and a 3-4 week moving average [6].
- A weekly difference of two noisy averages gives ADG; the break-even above (about 0.7 g/week) is small next to sampling noise. No source states the error of a mean from n shrimp (not found).
- Averages hide sex differences: females 1.88 vs males 1.42 g/week, harvested at 36.9 vs 31.5 g [3].
- Low ADG at high density can be density-driven: growth rose 0.95 to 2.6 g/wk after thinning, so slow growth is not always "end of growth" [3].
- Harvest needs more than size: molt stage, shell hardness, defects and taste decide the day [2]; Ecuadorian farmers are paid by packed size grade, not average [2].
- Profit follows biomass and survival, not size: weekly mortality of about 3 percent was seen, and survival estimates are hard [9][6].
- Growth responds strongly to temperature and size, so one threshold fits no pond [8].

## Still unknown or conflicting
- No primary source gives a numeric ADG or FCR cut-off for "harvest now"; [13] is the only ADG number and it is a vendor's.
- Target size differs: 16-30 g in Vietnam [1], 15-20 g in Thailand [14], 20-34 g in US studies [3][11]. Sources disagree because price steps and markets differ.
- Nothing found for the 4-10 day detection window the farmer reported, for disease nearby as a trigger, or for the error of an average from n shrimp.
- The Quang Ninh feed table [1] is a guide for hand feeding, not measured FCR; real daily feed at harvest size should come from the farmer's own records before the break-even is shown.
- Sources [4], [5] and [7] are old (1978-2002) and describe other systems and prices.
