# Size spread and sampling of whiteleg shrimp in one pond

Researched 2026-11-02. Question: how big is the spread of body size among individual vannamei at 10 to 40 g, how many shrimp per sample do sources advise, which sampling methods are biased, and does molting change length?

## Answer
- Weight CV of individuals is about 12 to 22 percent at 17 to 45 g (SD about 3.7 to 4.7 g, up to 7 g in large females). One pond study shows 34 percent at 12 g. [4][5][6][7]
- Length CV is about 4.5 to 7.5 percent at 100 to 170 mm. The only pond data at 130 mm: SD 8.0 mm (CV 6.2 percent), n = 30. [6][7]
- Best-supported length SD at 130 mm: X = 8 mm, plausible range 6.5 to 10 mm.
- FAO advises 50 to 100 shrimp per growth sample. No source found that states a precision such as plus or minus 5 percent. [3]
- Cast nets miss the biggest shrimp (sample too small); hand picking by new staff favours the biggest. [1][2]
- Molt stage shifts body length by about 1.5 to 1.9 percent (2 to 2.5 mm at 130 mm), measured on L. stylirostris, not vannamei. [8]

## Sampling bias
- Cast net: bigger shrimp escape the net, so the catch is biased to smaller sizes. One pond read 18 g the day before harvest and 20 to 21 g at drain-down, "always biased to the smaller sizes". [1][2]
- Hand picking: staff new to sampling "tend to bring back the largest animals" instead of random ones. Bias is to larger. [1]
- Feeding tray: no size bias found in any source. Dugger and Jory only say trays work for population estimates from feed use. [1] Not found.
- Pond edge: smaller shrimp stay in shallow areas, and low oxygen pushes shrimp to the side shallows, so edge samples lean to smaller shrimp. [1] FAO says shrimp are more abundant in corners and near the edge at night, with no size direction. [3]
- Also reported: cast net covers about 30 percent of its nominal area (Vietnamese reprint of [1], not a separate source), and different throwers give different results. [1]

## Worked line
SE of the mean = X divided by the square root of n. Growth is 0.3 mm per day, so about 0.9 mm in 3 days (1 mm used below). The difference between two independent samples has SE equal to SE times the square root of 2.

| n | SE of one mean, X = 8 mm | SE of the difference | Range X = 6.5 to 10 mm (one mean) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 10 | 2.5 mm | 3.6 mm | 2.1 to 3.2 mm |
| 30 | 1.5 mm | 2.1 mm | 1.2 to 1.8 mm |
| 50 | 1.1 mm | 1.6 mm | 0.9 to 1.4 mm |
| 100 | 0.8 mm | 1.1 mm | 0.7 to 1.0 mm |

With n = 10 the noise of one mean is 2.5 times the 3-day growth, and the noise of a difference is 3.6 times. Even n = 100 gives a difference SE about equal to the 1 mm growth. A 3-day change is not detectable per pair at n = 10 to 50. This assumes random sampling; the biases above add error that does not shrink with n.

## Molting
- On 1190 L. stylirostris (about 15 g), body length differed significantly between molt stages by 1.55 and 1.93 percent, and weight by 5.1 percent. Head length did not differ. [8] At 130 mm that is 2.0 to 2.5 mm, larger than 3-day growth.
- Shrimp in pre-molt and soft post-molt were lowest in weight. [8] Direction of the length effect by stage: not found in the text read.
- The same paper cites 11 to 13 days (hot season) and 18 to 22 days (cool season) between molts for 13 to 18 g stylirostris, and says up to 20 percent of shrimp in a pond may not molt. Both are quoted from other studies, not checked. [8]
- Day-to-day length change of one vannamei across a molt: not found. My arithmetic, not a source: 0.3 mm per day over 11 to 13 days is about 3 to 4 mm gained at one molt.
- A company guide says avoid sampling during molting periods. [9] Dugger and Jory list molting as a source of sampling error. [1]

## Unknown or conflicting
- Pond length data at 130 mm rest on one study with n = 30. An SD from 30 shrimp is itself uncertain by roughly plus or minus 13 percent.
- Length CV from [6] (6 to 11 percent) is higher than from [7] (4.5 to 6.6 percent). [7] comes from selected breeding lines, so a commercial pond may be wider; [6] is the closer match.
- Weight CV at 12 g is 34 percent in [6], well above the 12 to 22 percent at larger sizes.
- No source gives a recommended n for a stated precision, nor sample size for a 3-to-5-day growth comparison.
- No size-bias data for feeding trays; no source on tray-catch versus cast-net size in one pond.
- No measured size of the cast-net bias (only one 18 g versus 20 to 21 g example, a single anecdote).
- Whether a tray of about 10 laid-out shrimp is biased depends on who picks them: [1] says untrained pickers favour large ones.
- Molt effect on vannamei length, and the direction of the length effect by molt stage, not found.
